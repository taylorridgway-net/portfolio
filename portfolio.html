MathPath

My main project: a free, open-source platform for students who reach high school math with gaps in the foundations.

A free alternative to paid adaptive platforms

MathPath maps skills as a prerequisite graph. A short adaptive diagnostic finds where a student’s understanding breaks down, then it teaches that skill and checks for mastery before moving on.

The platform proposes mastery; the teacher confirms it with an independent paper assessment. Students log in with teacher-generated codes, so no names or emails are stored. It runs on Google Apps Script so it works on district Chromebooks where other hosts are blocked.

Status: the engine is being built and tested on a first set of skills, starting with fractions, before a pilot in my own classes.

Interactive tools

GC-84 graphing calculator

A browser graphing calculator with a familiar handheld-style keypad, so students can practice the same keystrokes they’ll use on test day.

AP Physics practice platform

Design for an AP Physics 1 practice site: diagrams, expression answers, hints, and solutions that unlock after real attempts.

Spring and pendulum simulators

Side-by-side simulations with live graphs that let students see that the period of simple harmonic motion depends only on certain physical parameters.

Used in AP Physics 1

AP Precalculus graph explorers

Quiz visuals with toggleable layers for increasing and decreasing intervals, concavity, and key points, plus an inverse trig explorer for domain restrictions.

Lessons and materials

Print-ready lessons in a consistent visual style, so students always know where to look.

Lesson series

Warm-ups, guided notes, homework, and answer keys for AP Precalculus, Integrated Math 3, and AP Physics 1, built in one design system so every lesson looks and works the same way.

Assessments that show reasoning

Quizzes split into core procedural questions and AP-level reasoning questions, so a score says what a student can do, not just how many they got right.

No-calculation concept questions
